# HC-8: Brake heat into the upright

_Hardpoints v1.7. Inputs are the current design's; generated by `scripts/handcalcs.py`. Work it on paper first, then check `answer_key.md`._

**Why:** requirement S-16. The cups' press fit is lost as the aluminium grows away from the steel, the cups' retaining compound and the 7075 temper have temperature limits, and nylon-insert nuts are allowed only below 80 C (FSAE T.8.3.2(c)).

**Given** (assumptions marked; `data/checks_week3b.json`):

- Car 290 kg; front brake share 0.751 of the braking force (LC3, 1.5 g, fixed bias).
- Disc 410 stainless, 180 mm outside, 112 mm inside, 4 mm thick; 7740 kg/m^3, c = 460 J/kg K, emissivity 0.6 (assumed).
- Hardest stop 100 km/h to rest (assumed). Endurance 22 km at a mean 50 km/h with 8 braking events per km, each 70 to 35 km/h (assumed). Ambient 30 C.
- Disc convection h = 30, 60, 100 W/m^2 K (no verified correlation: a range).
- Upright: surface 621 cm^2 (from the CAD solid), 0.778 kg, c = 960 J/kg K, h = 20 W/m^2 K (assumed). Hub flange: inboard face and rim 153 cm^2 at h = 30 (assumed; the outboard face is under the wheel). The hub and the upright are taken at one temperature: the bearings between them conduct well.
- Limits: Loctite 641 150 C; 7075-T73 long-term 120 C (assumed); nylon-insert nuts 80 C. Cup fit 13-45 µm interference at 20 C on the 68 mm bore (`notes/bearings.md`); expansion 23.6e-6 /K (7075) and 11.7e-6 /K (cup).
- Mounts: the solid steel hat of v1.7, 56-60 mm radius, 5.5 mm long, k = 25 W/m K; or a floating disc on 6 bobbins, each with two 8 mm contact faces and 6 mm of bobbin between them, in stainless (k 16) or titanium (k 6.7). Contact conductance 1000, 3000, 10000 W/m^2 K (assumed range).

**Find:** (1) the energy into one front disc in the hardest stop and its temperature rise with no cooling; (2) the mean braking power per front disc over the endurance; (3) the disc's running temperature for each h (convection + radiation = mean power); (4) the housing rise at which the cup fit opens; (5) the heat the upright and hub can shed at their limit, and the time constant against the endurance's length; (6) the thermal resistance the disc mount needs, and what each mount gives across the contact range.
