## HC-5: ARB bar, body and splined ends (S-09)

1. Body: tau = 16 T / (pi D^3) = 307.3 MPa (vehicle calcs 307.3); allowable 649 MPa: FoS 2.11.
2. End: Kt x 16 T / (pi Dm^3) = 203.5 MPa: FoS 3.19.
3. Minor diameter >= 17.9 mm for FoS 1.5. A spline cut into the 13.9 mm body would leave a minor diameter well under that, so the ends are upset: the bar is turned down from larger stock, leaving the 25 mm ends. S-09 PASS (>= 1.5). A static check of a ductile part takes no Kt at the shoulder; fatigue does (below).
4. Endurance in torsion: body 240 MPa (ground), spline 180 MPa (machined) (Se' = 0.5 Ftu, ka by surface, kb by size, kc 0.59, ke 0.814 for 99 % reliability).
5. Shoulder: r/d 0.288, D/d 1.80: Kt 1.16; tau_a 152 MPa: FoS 1.58. Spline: Kt 3, tau_a 87 MPa: FoS 2.08. S-23 PASS (>= 1.5). The full-warp torque is rare and is checked statically only.

## HC-6: Steering arm (DV-2)

1. I = 2 t h^3 / 12 = 32768 mm^4, A = 384 mm^2, M at S = 22.7 N m.
2. Section S, beam vs FEA:

| Edge [mm] | Beam [MPa] | FEA [MPa] | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13.7 | -9.6 | -7.9 | -17 % |
| -13.7 | 9.5 | 13.6 | +43 % |

   S is 10.9 mm from the load and the arm is 32 mm deep: well inside one depth, where Saint-Venant's principle says the bolt's local bearing field has not yet settled into the linear beam distribution. That is why no plain section exists (DV-2 stands).
3. Root of the upper ear (TR1): half the across-bolt force on a 17.8 mm cantilever, in-plane bending 6 P L / (t b^2) with Kt (R2, r/b 0.062, flat x 1.1) = 2.21: peak 40.3 MPa.
4. FEA steering-arm root peak (von Mises, LC6): 81.0 MPa; the plate model is -50 %.
   The FEA's peak sits where the arm joins the housing: the ring wall bends locally under the arm's moment, which a cantilever-ear model cannot see. So the ear-root model is not trusted on its own: HC-7 multiplies its stresses by FEA / model = 2.01 (never less than 1).

## HC-8: Brake heat (S-16)

1. Hardest stop: 0.5 m v^2 x share / 2 = 42.0 kJ into one front disc (0.483 kg): +189 K with no cooling.
2. Each endurance braking event puts 15.4 kJ into a front disc; 8 a km at 50 km/h is a mean 1.71 kW per front disc.
3. Mean running temperature, between stops (disc area 335 cm^2, both faces and the rim): h 30: 706 C, h 60: 584 C, h 100: 452 C. Small solid discs run hot; h = 60 is used below.
4. The fit loses 0.81 µm per K of housing rise: the loosest cup is free at +16 K, the tightest at +56 K. Above that the cups are held by the Loctite 641 alone, so its 150 C limit, not the fit, bounds the housing.
5. Housing limit 120 C (the lower of Loctite 641 and the assumed 7075 limit) with all-metal lock nuts on the upright; 80 C if any nylon-insert nut sits on it. Upright and hub shed (h A)_upright + (h A)_hub = 1.70 W/K, so 153 W at the limit (85 W at 80 C). The time constant m c / h A = 7 min (the upright's mass only) against a 26 min endurance: steady state is reached, so the run's length gives no relief.
6. The mount must hold the heat to 153 W across (584 - 120) K: R >= 3.03 K/W. The solid hat of v1.7 gives 0.15 K/W (margin 0.05): no thermal break at all. A floating disc on six bobbins, across the contact range stainless 7.87, 3.45, 1.91; titanium 9.60, 5.18, 3.63 K/W. Not counted: the caliper's own path into its lugs (it adds heat).
**Result: S-16 PASS on the estimate** with the titanium bobbins (D23): 3.63 K/W even at the high end of the contact range, margin 1.20 (1.71 at 3000 W/m^2 K). Stainless bobbins would fail at that end. The estimate leans on assumed h and contact values: the test plan measures the housing temperature after an endurance-length run.
