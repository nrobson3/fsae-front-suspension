# Requirements, decisions and change log: FSAE-class front suspension

**Project:** Portfolio Project 1, an independent design by Navy Robson, Mechanical Designer (engineering student).
**Revision:** E, 2026-10-26, for hardpoints v1.7 (after the second, improvement-focused Week 2 audit). Rev D covered v1.6, Rev C v1.5, Rev B v1.4, Rev A v1.0-v1.2.
**Independence:** public references only. No FSAE team's data, hardpoints or CAD.
**How it was made:** Navy set the brief and the four main design choices (D1-D4) and reviews each checkpoint; the analysis code, CAD scripts and the detailed technical decisions below were produced with an AI coding assistant (Claude), each with its reason recorded here. "Audits" in this document are independent AI review agents; every finding was re-checked before it was acted on.

**How this document is organised**
- It holds the reasoning: decisions, reference vehicle, derived requirements, change log, deviations, definitions and sources.
- The status of every requirement lives in the generated [requirements_matrix.md](requirements_matrix.md). That file is built from the analysis outputs by `python scripts/run_all.py`, so its numbers cannot drift from the model.

## 1. Design decisions

Numbers inside a decision are as of that decision; later decisions and the generated matrix (`notes/requirements_matrix.md`) hold the current values.

| D1 | Spring/damper actuation | Pushrod, rocker and inboard coilover | Less unsprung mass. The motion ratio is designed rather than accepted, and the anti-roll bar can be driven off the rockers. This is the layout most competitive FSAE cars use. | Outboard coilover on the lower wishbone (picture 1): heavier unsprung, fixed motion ratio, no clean ARB drive. |
| D2 | Axle | Front | Steering brings bump steer, Ackermann and kingpin geometry into the design. | Rear: no steering story. |
| D3 | Wheel size | 10 in | Lower mass and inertia. Packaging the brake and upright is harder, and that is part of the work. | 13 in: easier packaging, heavier. |

**Method.** Each case is solved at its loaded pose and at static, and the checks keep each member's tension and compression extremes. ## 4. Derived requirement: steering lock for the hairpin

- **Rule:** the FSAE autocross hairpin has a minimum outside diameter of 9 m (D.11.1.1(d)).
- **Radius:** the outer tyre edge must stay within 4.5 - 0.25 m driving margin = 4.25 m. The contact-patch path radius is then 4.25 - 0.0925 = 4.16 m.
- **Angle:** with the low-speed turn centre on the rear-axle line, δo = asin(L / R) = asin(1.55 / 4.16) = **21.9 deg** at the outer wheel.
- **Steering stop:** sits 0.5 deg beyond that (RC-16; 2 deg until v1.4, 1 deg in v1.5-v1.6).

**Why 45 % Ackermann.** In an autocross corner the lightly loaded inner tyre peaks at a smaller slip angle, so full Ackermann would drag it past its peak. Parallel steer would scrub the inner tyre in the tight hairpins and slaloms, where geometry dominates. Partial Ackermann in the 40-60 % band is the common compromise for a tight-course car. The design started at 50 % and moved to 45 % (RC-7) because the lower inner-wheel lock keeps the rim lip clear of the lower wishbone.

## 6. Deviations (requirements not met, accepted with reasons)

**DV-1: roll-centre migration in heave (K-08a).**

The RC height above ground moves up to 35.2 mm from its static height over ±25 mm (74.3 / 39.1 / 4.0 mm at -25 / 0 / +25 mm), against ±15 mm.
- **Cause (restated in the step E audit): no layout or chassis width tried reaches the target.** The trade study's best stays near 27 mm at any camber recovery from 39 to 81 % (`data/tradeoff_rc_camber.csv`), even with the upper pickups 270 mm out; the earlier wording named the pickups' width as the cause of the miss, which it is not. The width is why this design sits at 35 mm rather than near 27: With the rim-clearance rule at full lock and the upper pickups at y = 209 mm (29 mm outboard of the assumed chassis rail), the best any wishbone geometry achieves is 35.4 mm, and v1.6 sits on that limit (`data/tradeoff_rc_chassis_width.csv`, `plots/k08` panel b). Each extra 10 mm of pickup width buys about 1 mm.
- **Fix would need:** even pickups at y = 270 mm (an upper frame about 540 mm wide at the axle, or 90 mm outriggers) only reach 27-29 mm. With the rim clearance rule, no width tried reaches 15 mm (panel a). The 330 mm point lies slightly above the 270 mm one because that search did not fully converge.
- **Withdrawn claim:** Rev B/C said a 660 mm-wide frame would reach 15 mm. That came from a study without the rim constraint, and the v1.5 optimiser had left migration out of its cost. The Week 2 close audit found both; the frontier above was re-run inside the design's real constraints.
- **Effect:**
  - At the baseline ARB setting, front TLLTD moves from 52.0 % to 49.9 % at +25 mm and to 54.1 % at -25 mm (`notes/vehicle_calcs.md`). This moves only the front roll centre, with the rear assumed fixed: a sensitivity, not a prediction (in braking pitch the rear adds to it). It is about one bar step.
  - The CG-to-RC roll arm changes by only 10.2 mm.
  - The RC reaches ground level near full bump (4.0 mm at +25, -3.0 mm at +30). Jacking reverses there, but only in a pothole-scale event.
- **Accepted** for the conventional chassis assumed here. A target measured relative to the body is the candidate for a next car, through a recorded requirement change; T-06 (a tilt table at two ride heights) measures the shift.

**Action item (not a deviation): V-05, spring seating.** With the 150 lb/in spring (D21, 2.91 Hz) the static wheel deflection is 24.7 mm; the damper top-out stops droop at 27.0 mm, so the main spring goes slack over the last 2.3 mm (1.1 mm with v1's 25 N/mm spring). A light helper (tender) spring goes into the Week 3 BOM.

**Basis of the 5 mm moving clearance (V-07).** Worked on the tightest pair, the rim lip against the lower wishbone's rear leg: 6.4 mm at full bump and full lock (v1.7).

| Source | Effect on the gap |
|---|---|
| Rim inner profile: Braid publishes none, so the barrel is modelled with a 6 mm wall | ±2.0 mm |
| Steering stop position, ±0.2 mm of rack (the gap falls about 2 mm per mm of rack beyond the stop) | ±0.4 mm |
| Rack and column play past the stop, about 0.25 mm of rack | 0.5 mm |
| Wishbone weld distortion (jigged) | ±0.5 mm |
| Chassis pickup position (welded frame) | ±1.0 mm |
| Camber adjustment (upper wishbone rod ends since D20; shims before), ±0.5 deg about the lower ball joint | ±0.6 mm |
| Elastic deflection of the links, hub and upright on the bump stop | about 0.3 mm |
| Rim width and offset tolerance | ±0.3 mm |
| **Worst case all together / root-sum-square** | **5.6 / 2.5 mm** |

So at 6.4 mm the parts stay apart even if every source goes the wrong way at once (rev D listed only five sources, 3.8 mm, against a 5.2 mm gap; the second audit added the rest, and the stop margin went from 1 to 0.5 deg). A Monte Carlo on the real tolerances was planned and cut when Week 3b was re-ordered (RC-28): the worst-case stack-up stands, which is the conservative bound. Action: get Braid's inner barrel drawing before ordering and re-run `scripts/rim_clearance.py`.

**DV-2: no hand-calc check of the FEA on the upright itself (S-06f).**
- **What fails:** the plan's check, FEA within 10 % of a hand calc at a plain section of the upright. At the steering-arm ears in LC6 the FEA and the beam formula differ by -17 / +43 % (`notes/upright_fea.md`).
- **Cause:** the upright has no plain section. Its longest free arm (the steering arm) is about 20 mm long and 32 mm wide, so every section is within the load-introduction and root zones, where beam theory does not apply.
- **Cost:** no direct check of the upright model's stress level by an independent method.
- **What stands in for it:** the method validated on a slender pin-loaded bar with the same modelling (0.04 %, S-06c); the run stops unless the loads balance and every loaded face's area matches; mesh convergence (S-06d). Hand-calc packet HC-6 (`notes/handcalcs/`) reproduces the comparison on paper, shows why the section is too close to the bolt (10.9 mm into a 32 mm deep arm: inside the Saint-Venant zone) and checks the ear root with Peterson's Kt instead. The ring and stress-concentration benchmarks have since been done (S-06g, S-06h, RC-31), and a strain-gauged proof test is in the test plan (T-04).

**DV-3: upright fatigue screen (S-07a).**
- **What fails:** unlimited life at the peak load. Goodman FoS 1.12 for 1.6 g left-right cornering (LC2 <-> LC2i) at the housing's inboard bore edge, and 1.36 for 1.5 g braking against static.
- **Cause:** 7075 has no endurance limit. The screen asks every peak cycle to be survivable 5e8 times, which no aluminium part sized for its static loads gives.
- **Cost:** none in service. The screen is a bound, not the duty.
- **Accepted because:** S-07c, Miner's rule over the duty spectrum (3,000 km, cycle counts in `notes/duty_loads.md`, press-fit mean stress included), gives a factor of 3.24 on every stress before the life is used up (4.36 at the hole edges). If logged data shows a harsher duty, re-run `scripts/fea/upright_fatigue.py` with it.

**DV-4: upright over the 0.6 kg target (M-02).**
- **What fails:** 0.778 kg, one piece since v4 (D20; v3 was 0.845 kg as upright 0.746, bolted upper clevis 0.061 and steel shims 0.037), against 0.6 kg.
- **Cause:** the target came from the Week 3a plan, which assumed the layout's 71908 angular-contact pair. The tapered pair the static load needs (D13) sets a 68 mm bore with a 9 mm wall: the housing ring alone is 0.27 kg. The rest is sized by the FEA's load paths (post 0.15, caliper plate 0.13, lower web 0.12, ears 0.14 kg; `cad/upright/upright_build.json`).
- **Cost:** 0.18 kg more unsprung mass per corner than planned, about 1.8 % of the corner.
- **Effect:** M-01 still passes (10.2 kg against 11 kg). v3 took 0.024 kg out (the flat-bottomed lower web) and v4 0.067 kg (the one-piece upper clevis, D20). Pocketing the caliper plate and the web is the next lever if a lighter corner is wanted, at the cost of machining time and an FEA re-run.

## 7. Definitions used in the analysis

- **Camber recovery:** recovery = -(Δcamber of the outside wheel relative to the body) / (body roll angle) x 100 %, for the wheel in bump.
- **Ackermann %:** (δi - δo) / (δi,100 - δo), with cot δi,100 = cot δo - t / L at the actual outer angle δo (t = 1220 mm, L = 1550 mm).
- **Roll-centre height:** above ground, kinematic. RC height = half-track x (lateral contact-patch scrub rate). In roll it is the intersection of the two contact-patch-to-instant-centre lines.
- **Motion ratio:** damper compression per unit of wheel bump. Wheel rate = spring rate x MR².
- **Travel envelope:** kinematics and clearances are swept over ±30 mm. Physically, bump ends at +28.1 mm (6 mm bump rubber solid) and droop at 27.0 mm (damper top-out), so both ends of the sweep are a conservative extra 2-3 mm.
- **Interference rules:** see `scripts/interference.py`. Designed joints are declared per body (for example the upright may touch the wishbone's ball-joint node, never its legs); everything else must clear by 5 mm while moving, or by 3 mm inside the wheel.
- **FoS conventions:**
  - Yield FoS uses 4130 N Fty x 0.8 weld knock-down, 348 MPa.
  - Catalogue ratings given only as ultimate are held to 2.25, i.e. 1.5 on a limit load taken as ultimate / 1.5.
  - Bolts are sheared on the thread root.
  - Columns are pinned-pinned, Euler or Johnson by slenderness, and also checked as a stepped column with the rod-end shanks.
